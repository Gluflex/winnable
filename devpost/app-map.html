What the app does

Winnable lists every open Devpost hackathon and ranks it by your odds of winning cash. Three pieces do the work:

  • Collector (collector/collect.py): runs daily, reads the Devpost API and every hackathon page, keeps only cash prizes, and fits how many registrants really submit. It writes one file, data/hackathons.json.
  • Ranking (site/js/rank.js): pure functions that turn that file plus your profile into odds, expected cash and reasons you cannot enter. Tested in Node.
  • Board (site/js/app.js): the page you see. Draws rows, the detail receipt and the "How the odds work" chart. Your profile stays in the browser's local storage.

One action, end to end

The action: open About you, pick a country, age and student status, press Show my odds. The board re-sorts and some hackathons move to "Not for you" with a reason.

1The button saves your profile and redraws

site/js/app.js, search openAbout, then $('#pfDone').onclick

openAbout() builds the About you panel. Every change calls update(), which writes the profile to local storage with save(PKEY, profile). Show my odds closes the panel and calls render(true); the true turns on the row animation.

The handler 
$('#pfDone').onclick = async () => {
  update();
  closePanel();
  render(true);
  await loadGithub();
};

2The odds and the reasons are worked out

site/js/rank.js, search export function rankAll, export function odds, export function ineligibility

rankAll() runs two functions per hackathon. odds() projects registrants to the deadline (projectedRegistrants), converts them to expected submissions with the calibration a × registrants^b (expectedSubmissions), and divides cash prize slots by that. ineligibility() returns plain reasons such as "Not open in Brazil" or "In person: Singapore". No reasons and open long enough: the row goes to the ranked list; otherwise "Not for you" or "Opening soon".

The odds in one place

3The result is drawn

site/js/app.js, search function render, function rowHtml, function openDetail

render() calls rankAll(DATA, profile, new Date(), sort), writes each row with rowHtml() and the headline with summary(). Clicking a row runs openDetail(), which prints the same numbers as a receipt: registered today, projected, expected to submit, cash prizes, chance, expected cash.

A practice to reuse

Name the one assumption the product depends on, and test it against real data early. Every ranking here rests on one guess: how many registrants actually submit. devpost/spec.md, section "Decisions and Open Issues", records it as the genuine uncertainty, with five finished hackathons giving rates from 0.24 to 0.53. The build then fitted the curve on 150 finished hackathons (calibrate() in collector/collect.py) and shows the scatter in "How the odds work", so the spread is visible instead of hidden.

The same habit caught the plan's gaps. The first real data put a hackathon that opened that day at the top on four registrants; the fix (project the field from the elapsed share of the period, hold back hackathons under a fifth through) is recorded under "Revisions" in devpost/checklist.md and pinned by the projectedRegistrants tests in tests/test_rank.mjs.

On another project: write the riskiest assumption into the spec, check it on a handful of real cases before building on it, and when real data proves the plan wrong, record the revision and add a test that keeps the fix in place.
